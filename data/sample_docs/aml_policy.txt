ANTI-MONEY LAUNDERING (AML) POLICY
Document ID: AML-POL-001
Version: 3.2
Effective Date: January 1, 2024
Last Reviewed: December 15, 2023

SECTION 1: PURPOSE AND SCOPE

1.1 Purpose
This Anti-Money Laundering Policy establishes the framework for detecting,
preventing, and reporting money laundering activities within the organization.
The policy ensures compliance with the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA), the USA PATRIOT
Act, and applicable FinCEN regulations.

1.2 Scope
This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and third-party service
providers who handle financial transactions, customer due diligence, or
compliance monitoring functions. All business lines including retail banking,
commercial banking, wealth management, and payment processing are covered.

SECTION 2: CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE (CDD)

2.1 Standard Due Diligence Requirements
All new customers must undergo standard due diligence before account opening.
The following information must be collected and verified:
- Full legal name (individual or entity)
- Date of birth (for individuals) or date of formation (for entities)
- Government-issued identification number (SSN, EIN, or equivalent)
- Physical address and mailing address
- Source of funds and anticipated transaction patterns
- Nature of business or employment

2.2 Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)
Enhanced due diligence is mandatory for high-risk customers including:
- Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) and their immediate family members
- Customers in high-risk jurisdictions identified by FATF
- Correspondent banking relationships
- Money service businesses (MSBs)
- Customers with complex ownership structures or shell companies

For EDD customers, additional verification includes beneficial ownership
identification for all entities with 25% or greater ownership interest,
senior management approval before account opening, and quarterly review cycles.

2.3 Beneficial Ownership
Effective May 11, 2018, the Beneficial Ownership Rule requires financial
institutions to identify and verify the identity of beneficial owners of
legal entity customers. This applies to all legal entity customers opening
new accounts except for certain exempted entities.

SECTION 3: TRANSACTION MONITORING

3.1 Automated Monitoring Systems
The organization uses automated transaction monitoring systems to identify
potentially suspicious activity patterns. The systems apply rule-based
scenarios and behavioral analytics to flag unusual transactions.

3.2 Suspicious Activity Indicators
The following transaction patterns require enhanced scrutiny:
- Cash transactions at or structured to avoid the $10,000 CTR threshold
- Rapid movement of funds through multiple accounts within 24 hours
- Transactions inconsistent with customer's stated business purpose
- Unusual wire transfer patterns to high-risk jurisdictions
- Multiple customers sending funds to a single beneficiary
- Dormant accounts suddenly showing significant activity

3.3 Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs)
Financial institutions must file a Currency Transaction Report (CTR) for
any cash transaction exceeding $10,000 in a single business day, regardless
of whether the transaction appears suspicious. CTRs must be filed within
15 calendar days of the transaction date using FinCEN Form 112.

SECTION 4: SUSPICIOUS ACTIVITY REPORTING

4.1 Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs)
When a suspicious transaction is identified, employees must complete an
internal Suspicious Activity Report within 24 hours of discovery. The
compliance team reviews all internal SARs and makes the determination
to file a SAR with FinCEN within 30 calendar days of detection.

4.2 SAR Filing Thresholds
SARs must be filed for transactions involving $5,000 or more when there
is reason to suspect the transaction involves funds from illegal activity,
is designed to evade BSA requirements, or lacks a lawful purpose.

4.3 Tipping Off Prohibition
Employees are strictly prohibited from disclosing the existence of a SAR
or any investigation to the subject of the report. Violation of this
prohibition may result in criminal penalties and immediate termination.

SECTION 5: SANCTIONS COMPLIANCE

5.1 OFAC Screening
All customers and transactions must be screened against the Office of
Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list
and other applicable sanctions lists. Screening must occur at onboarding
and on an ongoing basis as lists are updated.

5.2 Blocked and Rejected Transactions
When a potential match to a sanctions list is identified, the transaction
must be blocked immediately and reported to OFAC within 10 business days.
The compliance team must conduct a thorough investigation before releasing
any blocked funds.

SECTION 6: TRAINING AND AWARENESS

6.1 Mandatory Training
All employees must complete AML compliance training annually. New employees
must complete training within 30 days of hire. Training covers recognition
of suspicious activity, reporting procedures, and regulatory requirements.

6.2 Specialized Training
Employees in high-risk roles including front-line staff, compliance officers,
and investigators receive specialized training covering advanced typologies,
investigation techniques, and regulatory updates.

SECTION 7: RECORD RETENTION

7.1 Retention Requirements
All AML-related records must be retained for a minimum of five years from
the date of creation. This includes customer identification records, SAR
filings, CTR filings, transaction records, and training completion records.
Records must be readily accessible for regulatory examination upon request.

SECTION 8: GOVERNANCE AND OVERSIGHT

8.1 BSA/AML Officer
The organization designates a qualified BSA/AML Compliance Officer who is
responsible for the day-to-day oversight of the AML program. The BSA/AML
Officer reports directly to the Chief Compliance Officer and has direct
access to the Board of Directors.

8.2 Independent Audit
The AML program is subject to independent audit by the Internal Audit
function or an external auditor no less than annually. Audit findings are
reported to the Audit Committee of the Board of Directors.

8.3 Board Reporting
The BSA/AML Officer provides quarterly reports to the Board of Directors
covering program effectiveness, significant cases, regulatory changes,
and key risk indicators.
