GENERAL DATA PROTECTION REGULATION (GDPR) COMPLIANCE FRAMEWORK
Document ID: GDPR-FRM-002
Version: 2.1
Effective Date: May 25, 2018 (Updated: March 1, 2024)
Last Reviewed: February 28, 2024

SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION AND LEGAL BASIS

1.1 Regulatory Context
The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is Regulation (EU) 2016/679,
which became enforceable on May 25, 2018. This regulation governs the
processing of personal data of individuals within the European Union and
European Economic Area. Organizations processing EU resident data must
comply regardless of their geographic location.

1.2 Key Definitions
Personal Data: Any information relating to an identified or identifiable
natural person ('data subject'), including name, identification number,
location data, online identifier, or factors specific to physical,
physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity.

Special Categories of Personal Data: Data revealing racial or ethnic origin,
political opinions, religious beliefs, trade union membership, genetic data,
biometric data for unique identification, health data, or data concerning
a natural person's sex life or sexual orientation.

Data Controller: The natural or legal person who determines the purposes
and means of processing personal data.

Data Processor: A natural or legal person who processes personal data on
behalf of the controller under a Data Processing Agreement (DPA).

SECTION 2: PRINCIPLES OF DATA PROCESSING

2.1 Lawfulness, Fairness, and Transparency
Personal data must be processed lawfully, fairly, and in a transparent
manner in relation to the data subject. Processing is lawful only when
at least one of the following legal bases applies:
- Consent: The data subject has given clear, affirmative consent
- Contract: Processing is necessary for contract performance
- Legal obligation: Processing is required by law
- Vital interests: Processing is necessary to protect life
- Public task: Processing is necessary for official functions
- Legitimate interests: Processing serves legitimate interests without
  overriding the rights of the data subject

2.2 Purpose Limitation
Personal data must be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate
purposes and not further processed in a manner incompatible with those
purposes. Further processing for archiving in the public interest,
scientific or historical research, or statistical purposes is permissible
with appropriate safeguards.

2.3 Data Minimization
Personal data collected must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what
is necessary in relation to the purposes for which it is processed.
Organizations must regularly review data holdings and delete data that
is no longer necessary.

2.4 Accuracy
Personal data must be accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date.
Every reasonable step must be taken to ensure that inaccurate personal
data is erased or rectified without delay. Data subjects have the right
to request correction of inaccurate data.

2.5 Storage Limitation
Personal data must be kept in a form that permits identification of data
subjects for no longer than necessary for the purposes for which the data
is processed. Data retention schedules must be established and enforced.

2.6 Integrity and Confidentiality
Personal data must be processed in a manner that ensures appropriate
security, including protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing
and accidental loss, destruction, or damage, using appropriate technical
and organizational measures.

SECTION 3: DATA SUBJECT RIGHTS

3.1 Right of Access (Article 15)
Data subjects have the right to obtain confirmation of whether their
personal data is being processed, and if so, to receive a copy of the
data along with information about processing purposes, data categories,
recipients, retention periods, and automated decision-making. Requests
must be fulfilled within one month, extendable by two months for complex
requests.

3.2 Right to Rectification (Article 16)
Data subjects have the right to have inaccurate personal data corrected
and incomplete data completed without undue delay.

3.3 Right to Erasure / Right to Be Forgotten (Article 17)
Data subjects have the right to request erasure of their personal data
when: data is no longer necessary; consent is withdrawn; data was
unlawfully processed; data must be erased for legal compliance; or
when data was collected in relation to offering information society
services to a child.

3.4 Right to Data Portability (Article 20)
Data subjects have the right to receive their personal data in a
structured, commonly used, and machine-readable format, and to transmit
that data to another controller, where processing is based on consent
or contract and is carried out by automated means.

3.5 Right to Object (Article 21)
Data subjects have the right to object at any time to processing of
personal data for direct marketing, processing based on legitimate
interests, and processing for scientific or historical research purposes.

SECTION 4: DATA BREACH NOTIFICATION

4.1 Internal Detection and Escalation
When a personal data breach is discovered, it must be escalated to the
Data Protection Officer (DPO) immediately, but no later than within 4
hours of detection. The DPO initiates the breach response procedure.

4.2 Supervisory Authority Notification
Without undue delay, and where feasible within 72 hours of becoming aware
of a personal data breach, the organization must notify the competent
supervisory authority unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk
to the rights and freedoms of natural persons.

4.3 Data Subject Notification
When a breach is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms
of natural persons, the data subjects must be notified without undue delay.
The notification must describe the breach in clear and plain language.

SECTION 5: DATA PROTECTION BY DESIGN AND BY DEFAULT

5.1 Privacy by Design
Technical and organizational measures must be implemented both at the time
of determining the means of processing and at the time of processing itself,
to ensure that GDPR principles are adhered to and data subject rights are
protected. This includes data minimization, pseudonymization, and access
controls built into systems from inception.

5.2 Privacy Impact Assessments (DPIA)
Data Protection Impact Assessments are mandatory before processing likely
to result in high risk, including: systematic and extensive profiling;
large-scale processing of special category data; systematic monitoring of
publicly accessible areas. DPIAs must be consulted with the DPO.

SECTION 6: INTERNATIONAL DATA TRANSFERS

6.1 Transfer Mechanisms
Transfers of personal data to third countries are only permitted when
adequate protection is ensured through:
- Adequacy decision by the European Commission
- Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) approved by the Commission
- Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs) approved by supervisory authorities
- Certification under an approved certification mechanism
- Codes of conduct with binding and enforceable commitments

6.2 Schrems II Implications
Following the Court of Justice of the EU ruling in Schrems II (C-311/18),
organizations must conduct Transfer Impact Assessments (TIAs) before
relying on SCCs for transfers to third countries, particularly the
United States. Supplementary measures may be required.

SECTION 7: DATA PROTECTION OFFICER

7.1 DPO Appointment
The organization has appointed a qualified Data Protection Officer (DPO)
who operates independently and reports to the highest management level.
The DPO is accessible to all employees and data subjects.

7.2 DPO Responsibilities
The DPO's responsibilities include: informing and advising on GDPR
obligations; monitoring compliance; providing advice on DPIAs; cooperating
with supervisory authorities; and acting as the contact point for
supervisory authorities and data subjects.
