SARBANES-OXLEY (SOX) INTERNAL CONTROLS FRAMEWORK
Document ID: SOX-CTL-003
Version: 4.0
Effective Date: January 1, 2024
Last Reviewed: December 20, 2023

SECTION 1: OVERVIEW AND REGULATORY BACKGROUND

1.1 Legislative Context
The Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002 (SOX) was enacted in response to major
corporate accounting scandals including Enron, WorldCom, and Tyco. The
Act established new standards for corporate governance, accounting,
and financial reporting for publicly traded companies listed on US
exchanges. Key provisions include Sections 302, 404, 906, and 409.

1.2 Applicability
SOX applies to all publicly traded companies registered with the SEC,
their subsidiaries and affiliates, and accounting firms that audit
these companies. Private companies preparing for an IPO often adopt
SOX-equivalent controls to facilitate the transition.

1.3 Section 302 Certification Requirements
The Chief Executive Officer (CEO) and Chief Financial Officer (CFO)
must personally certify in each annual (10-K) and quarterly (10-Q) report
that they have reviewed the report and that the financial statements
fairly present the financial condition of the company. They must also
certify that they are responsible for establishing and maintaining
disclosure controls and procedures.

SECTION 2: INTERNAL CONTROL OVER FINANCIAL REPORTING (ICFR)

2.1 Management Assessment (Section 404a)
Management is required to assess the effectiveness of the company's
internal control over financial reporting (ICFR) as of the end of each
fiscal year. The assessment must be based on a recognized control framework
such as the COSO Internal Control — Integrated Framework (2013).

2.2 Auditor Attestation (Section 404b)
For accelerated filers and large accelerated filers, the registered public
accounting firm that audits the financial statements must also attest to
and report on management's assessment of ICFR. This requirement does not
apply to non-accelerated filers or emerging growth companies.

2.3 COSO Framework Components
The COSO framework comprises five interrelated components:
1. Control Environment: The tone at the top, governance structures, and
   the commitment to integrity and ethical values.
2. Risk Assessment: The process for identifying and analyzing risks to
   achieving organizational objectives.
3. Control Activities: The policies and procedures that help ensure
   management directives are carried out.
4. Information and Communication: Systems that support the identification,
   capture, and exchange of financial information.
5. Monitoring Activities: Ongoing evaluations and separate evaluations
   to ascertain whether controls are present and functioning.

SECTION 3: KEY FINANCIAL REPORTING CONTROLS

3.1 Access Controls
System access to financial applications must be restricted to authorized
personnel based on the principle of least privilege. Access reviews must
be conducted quarterly. Privileged access must be logged and monitored.
Segregation of duties (SoD) must be enforced to prevent any single
individual from initiating, authorizing, and recording transactions.

3.2 Segregation of Duties
The following functions must be performed by separate individuals:
- Transaction initiation vs. transaction authorization
- Recording transactions vs. custody of assets
- IT development vs. IT operations
- Accounts payable processing vs. check signing authority
- Bank reconciliation vs. cash handling
Compensating controls must be documented when SoD is not feasible due
to organizational size constraints.

3.3 Period-End Close Controls
The financial close process must include documented checklists for
journal entry review and approval, account reconciliation sign-off,
variance analysis and explanations, intercompany elimination procedures,
and management review of financial statements before release.

3.4 Journal Entry Controls
All journal entries above defined materiality thresholds require dual
authorization. Automated journal entries must be system-generated with
documented logic and approved by the Controller. Manual top-side
adjustments require CFO approval. All journal entries must reference
supporting documentation.

SECTION 4: IT GENERAL CONTROLS (ITGCs)

4.1 Change Management
All changes to financial systems must follow the formal change management
process: change request, impact assessment, testing in non-production
environments, change advisory board approval, and post-implementation
review. Emergency changes are permitted with retrospective approval
within 24 hours.

4.2 Logical Access Management
User access provisioning and de-provisioning must follow defined procedures.
Terminated employees must have access revoked within 24 hours of termination.
Privileged accounts must use multi-factor authentication. Service accounts
must be inventoried and reviewed annually.

4.3 Computer Operations
Batch job scheduling and monitoring must ensure completeness and accuracy
of financial processing. Incident management procedures must address
failures in financial processing. Disaster recovery plans must be tested
annually, with recovery time objectives (RTOs) defined for critical
financial systems.

4.4 Program Development
System development lifecycle (SDLC) procedures must ensure segregation
between development, testing, and production environments. Code reviews
are required for all changes to financial applications. Security testing
must be conducted before deployment of significant system changes.

SECTION 5: ENTITY-LEVEL CONTROLS

5.1 Tone at the Top
The Board of Directors and senior management demonstrate commitment to
ethical behavior through a Code of Ethics applicable to all employees,
whistleblower hotline with non-retaliation protections, ethics training
for all employees, and annual certifications of code compliance.

5.2 Audit Committee
The Audit Committee of the Board must comprise independent directors and
include at least one financial expert as defined by SEC rules. The Audit
Committee oversees the engagement and independence of external auditors,
reviews significant accounting policies and estimates, and receives reports
from the Chief Audit Executive on internal audit activities.

5.3 Risk Assessment Process
Management conducts an annual enterprise risk assessment to identify and
prioritize risks to financial reporting objectives. The results inform
the scope of control testing and are reported to the Audit Committee.
Emerging risks, such as cybersecurity threats and changes in accounting
standards, are incorporated into the risk assessment.

SECTION 6: CONTROL DEFICIENCY CLASSIFICATION

6.1 Deficiency Types
Control deficiencies are classified based on their potential impact:

Control Deficiency: A deficiency exists when the design or operation of
a control does not allow management or employees to prevent or detect
misstatements in a timely manner. Does not require disclosure.

Significant Deficiency: A deficiency, or combination of deficiencies, that
is less severe than a material weakness yet important enough to merit
attention by those responsible for oversight of financial reporting.
Must be communicated to the Audit Committee.

Material Weakness: A deficiency, or combination of deficiencies, in ICFR
such that there is a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement
in the financial statements will not be prevented or detected on a timely
basis. Must be disclosed in the annual report (10-K).

6.2 Remediation Requirements
All significant deficiencies and material weaknesses must have documented
remediation plans with assigned owners, timelines, and status tracking.
Remediation progress is reported to the Audit Committee quarterly.

SECTION 7: SOX COMPLIANCE CALENDAR

7.1 Key Annual Milestones
Q1 (January-March):
- Complete prior year ICFR assessment and management report
- External auditor completes 404(b) attestation
- 10-K filing with SOX certifications

Q2 (April-June):
- Update risk assessment and scoping
- Begin current year control testing
- 10-Q filing with Section 302 certifications

Q3 (July-September):
- Midyear control deficiency assessment
- Remediation status review
- 10-Q filing with Section 302 certifications

Q4 (October-December):
- Complete control testing
- Management assessment of ICFR
- Year-end rollforward procedures
- 10-Q filing with Section 302 certifications
