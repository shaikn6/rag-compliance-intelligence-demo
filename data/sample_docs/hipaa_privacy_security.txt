HEALTH INSURANCE PORTABILITY AND ACCOUNTABILITY ACT (HIPAA) COMPLIANCE FRAMEWORK
Document ID: HIPAA-FRM-004
Version: 1.0
Effective Date: April 14, 2003 (Security Rule: April 20, 2005; Breach Rule updated: September 23, 2013)
Last Reviewed: January 15, 2024
Source: U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164; HITECH Act of 2009

SECTION 1: OVERVIEW AND SCOPE

1.1 Regulatory Context
The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (HIPAA)
establishes national standards for protecting individuals' health information.
The standards are implemented through the Privacy Rule, the Security Rule, and
the Breach Notification Rule, codified at 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164. Enforcement
is carried out by the HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR).

1.2 Covered Entities and Business Associates
The rules apply to covered entities: health plans, health care clearinghouses,
and health care providers who transmit health information electronically. Under
the HITECH Act of 2009, business associates that handle protected health
information on behalf of a covered entity are directly liable for compliance and
must enter into a Business Associate Agreement.

1.3 Protected Health Information
Protected Health Information (PHI) is individually identifiable health
information held or transmitted in any form. Electronic PHI (ePHI) is PHI created,
received, maintained, or transmitted in electronic form and is the subject of the
Security Rule.

SECTION 2: PRIVACY RULE

2.1 Minimum Necessary Standard
Covered entities must make reasonable efforts to use, disclose, and request only
the minimum amount of protected health information necessary to accomplish the
intended purpose.

2.2 Right of Access
Individuals have the right to inspect and obtain a copy of their PHI in a
designated record set. A covered entity must act on an access request no later
than 30 days after receipt, with one permitted extension of 30 days if the
individual is notified in writing of the reason for the delay.

2.3 Permitted Uses and Disclosures
PHI may be used or disclosed for treatment, payment, and health care operations
without individual authorization. Most other uses and disclosures require written
authorization from the individual.

SECTION 3: SECURITY RULE

3.1 Required Safeguards
Covered entities and business associates must implement administrative, physical,
and technical safeguards to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and
availability of all electronic protected health information.

3.2 Encryption
Encryption of ePHI is an addressable implementation specification. Where
encryption is not reasonable and appropriate, the entity must document that
determination and implement an equivalent alternative measure. ePHI that is
encrypted to HHS-specified standards is rendered unusable, and its breach does not
require notification.

3.3 Access Control
Technical safeguards must include unique user identification, emergency access
procedures, automatic logoff, and audit controls that record activity in systems
containing ePHI.

SECTION 4: BREACH NOTIFICATION RULE

4.1 Notification to Individuals
Following the discovery of a breach of unsecured protected health information, a
covered entity must notify each affected individual without unreasonable delay and
no later than 60 calendar days after discovery of the breach.

4.2 Notification to the Secretary
For a breach affecting 500 or more individuals, the covered entity must notify the
HHS Secretary without unreasonable delay and within 60 days. For a breach
affecting fewer than 500 individuals, the entity maintains a log and notifies the
Secretary annually, within 60 days after the end of the calendar year.

4.3 Notification to the Media
For a breach affecting more than 500 residents of a state or jurisdiction, the
covered entity must notify prominent media outlets serving that area, without
unreasonable delay and within 60 days.

SECTION 5: ENFORCEMENT AND PENALTIES

5.1 Civil Penalty Tiers
Civil monetary penalties are assessed in four tiers based on culpability: lack of
knowledge, reasonable cause, willful neglect corrected within 30 days, and willful
neglect not corrected. Penalty amounts per violation are adjusted annually for
inflation and are subject to an annual cap per identical provision.

5.2 Criminal Penalties
Knowingly obtaining or disclosing PHI in violation of HIPAA can result in criminal
penalties, including fines and imprisonment, enforced by the Department of Justice.
