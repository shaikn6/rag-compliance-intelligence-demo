CALIFORNIA CONSUMER PRIVACY ACT (CCPA/CPRA) COMPLIANCE FRAMEWORK
Document ID: CCPA-FRM-005
Version: 2.0
Effective Date: January 1, 2020 (CPRA amendments effective January 1, 2023)
Last Reviewed: January 20, 2024
Source: California Civil Code Section 1798.100 et seq.; California Office of the Attorney General (oag.ca.gov); California Privacy Protection Agency (cppa.ca.gov)

SECTION 1: OVERVIEW AND SCOPE

1.1 Regulatory Context
The California Consumer Privacy Act of 2018 (CCPA) grants California consumers
rights over their personal information. It was amended by the California Privacy
Rights Act of 2020 (CPRA), which took effect on January 1, 2023 and created the
California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) to implement and enforce the law
alongside the California Attorney General.

1.2 Covered Businesses
The law applies to for-profit businesses that do business in California, collect
California residents' personal information, and meet at least one threshold: annual
gross revenue over 25 million dollars; buying, selling, or sharing the personal
information of 100,000 or more consumers or households; or deriving 50 percent or
more of annual revenue from selling or sharing personal information.

1.3 Personal Information
Personal information is information that identifies, relates to, or could
reasonably be linked with a consumer or household. The CPRA adds a category of
sensitive personal information, which includes government identifiers, financial
account details, precise geolocation, and data revealing race, religion, health,
or sexual orientation.

SECTION 2: CONSUMER RIGHTS

2.1 Right to Know
A consumer has the right to request that a business disclose the categories and
specific pieces of personal information it has collected, the sources, the business
purpose, and the third parties with whom it is shared, over the preceding 12 months.

2.2 Right to Delete
A consumer has the right to request deletion of personal information the business
collected from them, subject to statutory exceptions.

2.3 Right to Correct
Under the CPRA, a consumer may request correction of inaccurate personal
information a business maintains.

2.4 Right to Opt Out of Sale or Sharing
A consumer has the right to direct a business not to sell or share their personal
information. Businesses that sell or share personal information must provide a clear
"Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link.

2.5 Right to Limit Use of Sensitive Personal Information
Under the CPRA, a consumer may direct a business to limit the use and disclosure of
sensitive personal information to what is necessary to provide the goods or services
requested.

2.6 Right to Non-Discrimination
A business may not discriminate against a consumer for exercising any CCPA right,
such as by denying goods or services or charging different prices.

SECTION 3: BUSINESS OBLIGATIONS

3.1 Response Timeline
A business must respond to a verifiable consumer request within 45 days of receipt.
The period may be extended by an additional 45 days, for a maximum of 90 days, when
reasonably necessary and the consumer is notified.

3.2 Privacy Policy
A business must maintain a privacy policy that describes consumers' rights and the
categories of personal information collected, disclosed, sold, or shared, and must
update it at least once every 12 months.

SECTION 4: ENFORCEMENT AND PENALTIES

4.1 Administrative Fines
The California Privacy Protection Agency and the Attorney General may impose
administrative fines of up to 2,500 dollars per violation and up to 7,500 dollars
per intentional violation or per violation involving the personal information of
consumers under 16 years of age.

4.2 Private Right of Action for Data Breaches
Consumers may bring a private action when nonencrypted and nonredacted personal
information is subject to a breach due to a failure to maintain reasonable security.
Statutory damages range from 100 dollars to 750 dollars per consumer per incident,
or actual damages, whichever is greater.
